## Launch event

Choose a date only after the production checkout and webhook are verified. Confirmed development tests do not establish production readiness.

- Two days before: choose a 30-minute slot, confirm the timezone, and invite interested contacts.
- One day before: publish the demonstration and event time. Prepare the existing Growth tab’s share assets.
- At the start: explain the price, replacement rule, and how to watch for free.
- During the event: show real takeovers as they happen. Ask owners before featuring or tagging them on your social account. Never expose their purchase email.
- At the end: share the actual results, including if no one purchased. Invite owners to use their public share cards.
- The next day: ask participating owners what confused them and review checkout drop-off.

Announcement draft:

> I’m running a 30-minute Take The Wall launch on [date], [time/timezone]. Watch one page change owners, or put your project up for $3.99. Your content stays until the next takeover replaces it. Come watch for free: https://takethewall.com/?utm_source=x&utm_medium=social&utm_campaign=first_owners&utm_content=launch_event

## Measurement and next decision

UTM links label campaigns; they do not by themselves implement purchase attribution. Use available VisitorPing campaign reports for traffic, verified live Stripe activations for paid totals, and the existing owner referral attribution for owner-shared links. Do not claim per-campaign purchase conversion unless the reporting actually connects those events.

Record each post’s date, public URL, tracking URL, observed visits, and questions received. Exclude test payments, owner self-tests, bot traffic, and demo additions. Leave unavailable metrics blank.

After seven days, compare what people did:

- Few visits: revise the opening/demo and where you share it.
- Visits but few previews: ask five visitors what they think the purchase provides.
- Checkout starts without payment: inspect actual errors and ask about purchase hesitation.
- Purchases but few shares: ask owners whether the existing share card is useful.

Change one thing at a time. Do not interpret tiny sample sizes as reliable conversion forecasts.
